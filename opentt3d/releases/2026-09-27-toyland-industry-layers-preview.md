# Toyland industry layers and registered gold soils

This preview brings the catalogue to **1,703 provisional voxel models**. It adds
independent Toyland oil-rig water and correctly registered gold-mine soil, plus
source-audited climate bindings for animated pools and rig/paper bodies.

## Play

The [verified `.24` desktop release](https://github.com/cstanislav/opentt3d/releases/tag/opentt3d-dev-20260927.24)
is available for Windows, macOS and Linux. This preview's executable packages appear
after its exact-commit release workflow passes. Required graphics are bundled; see
the [installation guide](https://github.com/cstanislav/opentt3d/blob/main/opentt3d/PLAYING.md).

## Artwork and ownership

- Gold soil sits below its independently owned floor, with all nine reviewed contacts
  free of intersections. Three pool aliases preserve the original animated colours.
- Rig water retains its full footprint and submerged height, with Toyland's separate
  teal flecks. Thirteen rig/paper body aliases follow source-proven palette animation.
- Every preceding model and binding is preserved. Original industry availability,
  construction, animation, helicopter service, commands and saves remain authoritative.

## Verification

The gold increment passes 16 prototype and six integrated controls, three negative
controls, 204 native unit cases, two script regressions and 142 asset tests. All 2,860
staged/integrated views agree exactly.

The rig/paper increment passes 18 prototype, two baseline and six integrated controls,
three negative controls and 142 asset tests. All 964 staged/integrated views agree.
Both actual helicopter controls preserve the oil rig's elevated landing plane.
Individual native layers agree between OpenGL and Vulkan; the retained one-pixel
joined initial-paper difference is reproduced in the preceding baseline.

## Still in progress

Final visual approvals remain zero. Source registration, detailed paint, larger-aircraft
clearances, remaining catalogue states and later fidelity passes remain open. These
short controls do not establish arbitrary-world smooth 60fps or long-duration memory
acceptance.
